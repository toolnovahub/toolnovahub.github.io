JPG vs PNG vs WebP: Which Format Should You Use?

Find the right image format for photos, screenshots, graphics, and transparent backgrounds.

How each format works

JPG uses lossy compression, making it a familiar choice for photographs. PNG uses lossless compression and can retain transparent pixels, which works well for screenshots, diagrams, and logos. WebP offers lossy and lossless encoding, including transparency, and is widely supported in modern browsers.

None of these formats is always the smallest. Content, image dimensions, compression settings, and encoder choices matter.

Practical examples

For an ordinary vacation photograph, JPG or WebP is usually a sensible starting point. For a logo with transparent corners or a screenshot with tiny text, PNG may preserve crisp edges better than a low-quality JPG.

For a website, compare WebP against JPG or PNG at a similar visual quality before deciding. Avoid converting every asset to one format blindly.

Conversion cannot restore lost quality

Turning a compressed JPG into PNG does not recreate details removed by JPEG. The PNG may even be larger. Repeated lossy conversions can reduce clarity further.

Transparent PNG pixels must be flattened onto a background for JPG output. ToolsNova uses white as that background when converting to JPEG.

Mistakes worth avoiding

Do not choose PNG just because the word lossless sounds better when your original is already a JPG. Do not heavily compress images of text. Keep original copies and check whether the target service supports your chosen format.
